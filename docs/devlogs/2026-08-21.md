## Decide before the work whether the frame has room for it

*2026-08-21 01:41*

The reactive throttle cured the freeze and left the tearing, and that split
follows from what it is: skipping frames lowers the average cost and leaves
the peak exactly where it was. Leene Square is passable because cumulative
starvation is an average problem. The world map still tears because a tear is
a single-frame problem -- it went from every frame to one in four, and one in
four is what a visible periodic wobble is made of.

So the decision moves in front of the work. ra_rc_frame_fits() is asked
whether the blanking left this frame can hold what the last evaluation cost,
and declines the frame if it cannot. The predictor is the last cost rather
than linesMax on purpose: a high-water mark is raised for good by one
expensive frame, such as the one where a trigger fires, and predicting from
it would decline every ordinary frame afterwards.

Two escapes, both load-bearing. A cost of zero always runs, which is also the
case the host suite lives in since RA_VCOUNT never advances there. And
starvation always runs -- RA_RC_FRAME_STARVE_MAX is 16, so a game whose own
handler never leaves room still evaluates about every seventeenth frame,
against the 8% of frames the old VCOUNT hook shipped on without missing an
unlock. A reader that never evaluates is the worse bug.

The reactive throttle stays alongside: the gate stops the overruns nobody had
to take, this pays back the ones starvation forced.

rcRoomMax takes the byte reserved at +0x87 since the struct was written, so no
offset moves. Read against rcLinesMax it says whether any of this can work --
above it, there are frames with room and the gate is the whole fix; at or
below it, no frame in the session could hold the work and the cost itself is
what is left to attack. Zero means the game's own handler had already run past
line 262 before ours started, which is a reading and not a gap.

## Divide the set instead of the calendar: 101 lines do not fit in 71

*2026-08-21 02:02*

Chrono Trigger's world map answered with the two adjacent bytes: rcLinesMax
101, rcRoomMax 70 of a possible 71. The game's own VBlank handler returns
almost immediately and leaves the reader essentially the whole blanking
period -- there was no room being stolen and none to reclaim. The work needs
40% more scanlines than a Nintendo DS frame spends not drawing, on the
reader's best frame.

That ends the scheduling approach. The budget is not a policy chosen badly;
no gate, throttle or skip counter can place 101 lines inside 71. And it is
not a Chrono Trigger quirk: 45 definitions cost 67 lines and 98 cost 101, so
anything past roughly seventy definitions is over the ceiling on any game.
Contra 4 has never torn because it is the last size that fits.

So the trigger loop is divided. rc_runtime_do_frame_slice() is a local patch
to the vendored rcheevos, with rc_runtime_do_frame() kept as a wrapper for
slice 0 of 1 parts so its own tests and any other caller see no change.
Modulo rather than a contiguous range, so changing parts mid-session cannot
leave a band of triggers unvisited for a cycle.

Memrefs are updated on every call and only the loop is divided, which is the
half that decides whether this is honest: each trigger still sees a true
one-frame delta whenever it is looked at, where whole-frame skipping updated
no memrefs at all on the frames it sat out. An earlier note rejected splitting
because it "would hand the delta operators two different notions of previous"
-- updating every memref every frame is what makes that false. Per trigger
the split is a lower sample rate and nothing else, which is what the throttle
already was; the difference is that this bounds the peak.

rcParts tunes itself and only ever rises. A step down would have to predict
what a larger slice costs, and rc_update_memref_values() runs on every call
whatever the slice, so a fixed share cannot be divided -- guessing it
optimistically is the oscillation this exists to end.

It also removes a hardware round-trip. rcParts at +0x69 says how much of the
101 lines was that undividable share: settled below the ceiling means the
division worked; pinned at RA_RC_PARTS_MAX with rcLinesMax still above
rcRoomMax means the memref pass alone does not fit and the peek path is what
is left to attack.

## Move the sliced frame out of the submodule and into our own source

*2026-08-21 02:05*

The previous commit put the slice loop in rcheevos, which is a submodule
pinned at RetroAchievements' v12.4.0. The parent records a gitlink, not the
files, so that edit belonged to no commit this project can make: it built here
and would be absent from every fresh clone -- the slicing would silently
revert to the unpatched library, or fail to link. Neither the host suite nor
the release script would have said a word. The working tree refusing to come
clean is what caught it.

So ra_rc_do_frame_slice() lives in ra_rcheevos.c now and the submodule is back
at its pinned commit, untouched. What is lost by not calling upstream's
function is what this fork has no use for: its loop raises nine event types
and ra_rc_event_handler() acts on one, ACHIEVEMENT_TRIGGERED, counting the
rest, and there are no leaderboards or rich presence here so those two loops
iterate zero times. What is kept is everything that changes behaviour --
memrefs updated first, triggers skipped when null or holding an invalid
memref, RESET read back off the trigger rather than treated as a state, and
events raised only on a real transition.

One narrowing, recorded because it changes what a snapshot field means:
rcEvents now counts trigger state transitions rather than nine event kinds.

## Measure the memref pass on its own; it is three quarters of the budget

*2026-08-21 02:22*

Two hardware readings on Chrono Trigger, world map and Leene Square: rcParts
8, rcLines 58 and 60, rcRoomMax 70, rcPeeks 237. It fits. The tearing is down
to roughly what the official release does and the text region is legible.

And rcLinesMax is the field that lies about it. It still says 100 because it
is a running maximum that is never reset, so it reports a frame from before
the tuner converged. The interpretation shipped one commit ago -- "pinned at
RA_RC_PARTS_MAX with rcLinesMax still above rcRoomMax means the memref pass
does not fit" -- is therefore not the reading it claimed to be. rcLines is,
and it fits.

The two costs give the split: fixed + var/1 = 101 and fixed + var/8 = 58, so
the trigger loop is ~48 scanlines and the memref pass ~53. Three quarters of
the available budget is spent before a single trigger is looked at, which is
the ceiling on slicing and the reason parts 8 wins so little over parts 4. So
rcMemrefLines measures it directly instead of inferring it from two
whole-frame readings on two different games. One of the three bytes reserved
at +0x25, because the bytes beside rcLinesMax are spent; no offset moves.

It also turns up a figure worth staring at. 237 peeks in ~53 scanlines is
about 950 ARM9 cycles per read, and 48 scanlines for 98 definitions is ~2,000
cycles each. Nothing in the peek path costs that. Both halves being uniformly
slow by a similar factor points at how this binary executes rather than what
it does -- it runs from DSi WRAM at 0x03740000, which a DS game's own MPU
setup has no reason to have marked cacheable. Documented as the next lever,
not acted on yet.

The parts ratchet is left alone deliberately. Tuning against rcRoomMax would
settle this set at 3, which is 69 scanlines against 70 -- the arithmetic
optimum with no margin. Parts 8 costs 58 and has eleven to spare, and a
picture at the level of the official release is worth more than the difference
between visiting a trigger at 7.5 Hz and at 20 Hz. It gets revisited when the
fixed term comes down.

## rcMemrefLines is 47: two thirds of the budget cannot be divided

*2026-08-21 02:33*

Measured on hardware, identical on both screens of the same session, against
an inference of ~53 from two whole-frame readings on two different games.
Close enough to leave the conclusion standing and precise enough to end the
argument: 47 scanlines of the 70 available belong to the memref pass, which
runs on every call whatever the slice.

So slicing is finished. rcParts 8 already spends only 11 scanlines on
triggers; doubling to 16 would save five and halve their detection rate. It
worked and this is where it stops.

Recorded with the cost stated plainly rather than left implicit: a trigger is
visited every eighth frame, so a hit-count condition counts eight times slower
and a transient a ResetIf or PauseIf wanted to see can pass between visits.
Nothing is lost from the queue, but "hold for 300 frames" becomes 2,400.

That is the argument for the cache lead, now the only lever left with a large
number behind it: 47 scanlines for 237 reads is ~840 cycles each. Cached, the
per-frame cost would land near 15, rcParts would go back to 1, and the caveat
above goes with it.

## Claim an instruction cache for the reader's window, and report what was found

*2026-08-21 02:41*

Two unrelated halves of the per-frame work -- 840 cycles per memory read, 2,000
per definition -- being slow by the same factor is not a property of what the
code does but of how it runs. This binary executes from DSi WRAM at 0x03740000,
which a DS game's own MPU has no reason to have marked cacheable; on a retail
DS there is nothing there.

Instruction cache only, and that asymmetry is the whole safety argument.
Cacheability is per region, and the region covering 0x03740000 may also cover
the I/O registers and the ROM cache the card DMAs into. A data cache over
either is fatal -- VCOUNT would stop advancing and cached ROM would go stale.
An instruction cache over the same region is inert, because nothing is fetched
for execution from an I/O register or a ROM cache line, and the only code there
is ours: written once by the bootloader before we run and never modified.

It also declines the one region it must not touch. If the winner also spans
0x02000000 it governs how the game's code is fetched -- nds-bootstrap's own MPU
patch can produce exactly that, widening a region to PAGE_128M at base 0 -- and
a loader that DMAs overlays into main RAM is the last program that should have
an instruction cache switched on underneath it. Reported, not touched.

raMpuBits at +0x26 is that report, in a reserved byte so no offset moves, and
it is a report rather than a boolean because there are four ways this comes
back and three of them send the diagnosis elsewhere. Bit 0x40 is read first: a
per-region bit does nothing while the cache itself is off.

The two decisions are pure functions -- ra_mpu_region_pick() over the eight raw
region registers and ra_mpu_report() over the three CP15 words -- so the suite
drives every branch, including the one that would have been a real bug: a size
field of 31 is the whole address space and 1u << 32 is undefined.

## The cache was never off: raMpuBits 0xD9, and the cost is the read itself

*2026-08-21 03:03*

Hardware refutes the hypothesis cleanly. Region 1 wins for 0x03740000 and was
already instruction-cacheable, is data-cacheable too, the instruction cache is
on globally, and nothing was changed -- nds-bootstrap's own MPU patch widens
region 1 to reach from main RAM through our window and that region is fully
cacheable. rcMemrefLines unchanged at 47, which is what "nothing was changed"
should look like. The ~845 cycles per read is what this code costs with both
caches on.

ra_icache_claim() stays: it reports, it declines the region that holds the
game's code, and on a game whose MPU puts our window in a region of its own
with the bit clear it would still do something. On the measured configuration
it does nothing, which is worth having written down.

The host then supplies the denominator the timing never had: 69 reads for
Contra 4's 56-definition set, all in the memref pass and none in trigger
evaluation. One rate explains both games -- Chrono Trigger's 237 reads in 47
measured scanlines is 845 cycles each, and Contra 4's 69 reads at that rate
leave 53 lines for 1,946 conditions, or 117 cycles each. 117 per condition is
sane. 845 for a four-instruction translate, two comparisons and a load is not.

So the cost is the read: scattered main RAM addresses, each a cache line fill
on the external bus, taken from inside the game's VBlank where the game's own
DMA holds that bus. Which also says why the trigger loop is fast -- it reads
memref values out of cached DSi WRAM, off the contended bus -- and why the cost
tracks distinct addresses rather than conditions, and why Contra 4 has never
torn: 69 addresses against 237.

rcMemrefMin takes the last reserved byte and decides it without a dedicated
run. Min far below max means contention, and then blanking is the worst place
to do this work rather than the best. Min equal to max exonerates the bus and
sends 845 cycles looking for a third explanation.

## There was never a 20x anomaly: the pass walks 3.5x what it reads

*2026-08-21 03:12*

rcMemrefMin 45 against rcMemrefLines 47, stable to 4% over a play session.
Bus contention varies with what the game is transferring; deterministic
arithmetic does not. So the bus is exonerated, and that is the third
hypothesis in a row to die to a measurement rather than an argument.

Then the host counted what the whole calculation had assumed. Contra 4's set:
68 memref entries, all typed, and 173 modified memrefs -- 241 entries walked
for 69 reads. rc_update_memref_values() has two loops and only the first reads
memory. The second calls rc_get_modified_memref_value(), which in the common
case touches no memory at all: it evaluates two operands, converts types and
combines them. That is where AddSource, SubSource and the arithmetic operators
live, and on the ARM9 that arithmetic is not cheap -- no integer divide
instruction, so / and % are libgcc calls, and no FPU, so a float definition
promotes the whole combine into soft-float.

Redone with entries instead of reads, Chrono Trigger's 47 scanlines come to
~239 cycles per entry for two operand evaluations, a conversion and a combine.
That is an ordinary number. The 845-cycles-per-read figure was an artefact of
dividing by the wrong count.

Which closes it. There is no large win here; the cost is the work. And it says
precisely what rcParts does not reach: slicing divides the trigger loop, hence
47 fixed plus 11 for triggers, while every memref is updated every call.

Slicing the memref pass too is not available, and the reason is worth keeping.
Whole-frame skipping was uniform -- everything stale by the same amount, so a
trigger comparing two addresses still compared one instant. Per-memref slicing
is not: a trigger would read some addresses from this frame and others eight
frames old, and a condition comparing them would compare different moments.
That is a correctness change rather than a sample-rate change.

So the number to watch for a future set is not the achievement count but the
count of memrefs and modified memrefs, which the host's list walk now reports
alongside the arena figures.

## Chrono Trigger runs: the reader's frame now fits the game's blanking period

*2026-08-21 03:17*

The game froze on entering Leene Square, every time, with no menu involved. It
now passes it and continues into the story, the world-map tearing is down to
what the official release does, and the character-text region that trembled
with Crono low on screen is legible. A compatibility gain on a game this fork
could not run, won by measurement -- every step is a number read off a 3DS with
the RAM viewer.

The cause was ours: the per-frame evaluation overran the game's VBlank, and
everything the game does after that interrupt returns was pushed into drawn
pixels. rcLinesMax 101 against rcRoomMax 70 of a possible 71 -- the game leaves
the reader nearly all its blanking and the work still needed 40% more than the
console has. No gate and no throttle can schedule that, so the trigger loop is
evaluated in self-tuning slices instead.

What it cost: a trigger is visited every eighth frame, inside the 8% of frames
this project already shipped on without missing an unlock. Nothing was
disabled, no achievement is lost, no other game changed.

Three hypotheses died to measurements along the way, and each is recorded with
the field that killed it: skipping frames lowers the average and leaves the
peak, so it cured the freeze and left the tearing; the window was never
executing uncached (raMpuBits 0xD9 -- already both instruction- and
data-cached); the bus was not stalling either (rcMemrefMin 45 against 47,
stable to 4%); and the alarming 845 cycles per memory read was a division by
the wrong count, since the memref pass walks 3.5x more entries than it reads
and most of them are arithmetic. Per entry the cost is an ordinary 239 cycles,
so there is no anomaly and no large win left -- which is what makes slicing the
fix rather than a workaround.

Five new snapshot fields, all in bytes reserved since the struct was written,
so no existing offset moves and the hardware checklist stays valid: rcRoomMax,
rcParts, rcMemrefLines, rcMemrefMin and raMpuBits.

## verbose_log in ra.cfg: a boot screen for playing, not for diagnosing

*2026-08-21 14:45*

The RA ladder narrated everything to the screen -- stage headings, the SCFG
registers, every line dsiwifi prints on its way up, a heap report between rungs
and a twenty-line summary. Several hundred lines. That is the right screen for
finding out why a boot failed and the wrong one for someone waiting to play.

verbose_log picks, and defaults to 0. That is the one default in the parser
that is not "behave exactly as before": a card with no opinion gets the quiet
screen and the verbose one is a diagnostic mode you ask for.

It governs the screen only. ra_wifi_launcher.log gets everything either way,
and the asymmetry is the point rather than a shortcut -- every hardware finding
in this project arrived through that file, and a setting that silenced it would
mean a reflash before anything could be looked at, which is the cost quiet mode
exists to remove. As it stands it is safe to leave on forever.

The quiet screen is ten steps named for what the player is waiting for rather
than the API call underneath, a bar, and the essentials underneath once known.
Those are written from `verdict`, the same structure the verbose summary comes
from, so the two cannot disagree, and they are ordered by what a failing boot
needs first: "Could not sign in" at the top of the block is the whole diagnosis
for the most common problem this loader will ever have. A spinner turns while a
rung blocks, because association can take forty seconds and a stopped run
otherwise looks exactly like a waiting one.

Four things about drawing on a DS console that took more thought than the
feature:

The config is read before anything is printed. verboseLog decides what the
screen is for, so it cannot be learned at stage 0c with three stages already
drawn. Stage 0c still reports every field, and the property that made the old
ordering right is kept -- the file is still parsed with no radio up.

Cursor positioning goes through PrintConsole's own fields, not an ANSI escape.
libnds does implement the position escape and nothing in this tree has ever
used it, so no build here has proved it works on this console, and a progress
display is a poor place to find out. cursorX and cursorY are documented public
fields and are what the escape would set.

Rows are relative to wherever the cursor already was, and the base is clamped.
Nothing clears the screen before the probe runs, and writing past the last row
scrolls the console -- which would move every row already addressed.

Padding is counted in cells, not bytes. Rows are padded to the console width
rather than erased, for the same reason as the escape above, and \x1b[31m is
five bytes occupying no cell: padding by strlen() leaves the tail of the
previous line, and a 38-byte coloured line truncated at 32 loses its closing
escape and leaves the console yellow for everything after it.

raWifiBar() and raWifiVisible() live in ra_screen.c for the reason
ra_wifi_verdict.c exists: the rest of ra_wifi.c needs a console, a FIFO or a
socket and these need sniprintf. Not ceremony over four lines of division -- a
progress bar's bugs are invisible. A full bar beside "95%" reads as a fault in
the loader, a bar that stops at 90% reads as a hang, and neither would fail a
build. Both ends and the rounding are pinned on the host, as is the
unterminated-escape case.

## Pulse one dot instead of spinning a stick

*2026-08-21 23:54*

The rotating - \ | / reads as corruption at this resolution: four glyphs of
different widths flickering next to a word. Pulsing one dot -- . o O o --
changes the size of a mark that never moves, which is the shape of a progress
animation; a spinning stick is the shape of a terminal.

Symmetric, so it returns through 'o' rather than snapping from 'O' back to '.'
and breathes instead of ticking. Every sixteenth frame rather than every
eighth, about one breath a second, because faster reads as flicker again --
which was the original complaint.

The frame choice is pure and pinned on the host, including the wrap across a
whole byte of ticks. A wrong mask here prints a space, and a spinner that stops
is exactly what this exists to rule out: a stalled run looking identical to a
waiting one.
